# Keeping performance in a coherent filmed space

Read this guide for multi-person blocking, changing camera angles, moving-camera shots, lighting continuity or conflicts among references and controls. It coordinates the five roles around the approved scene. Detailed acting belongs in [actor performance](actor-performance-and-facial-actions.md); dialogue angle construction belongs in [dialogue coverage](dialogue-coverage-grammar.md).

## Establish what must remain true

Begin with the directing decisions relevant to this shot: who is present, where the action occurs, what changes, and what the next shot must be able to continue. Do not create a second complete production ledger; use the owners and links in [project workflow](project-workflow.md).

Keep the set and its current image distinct. A doorway can remain beside a table while appearing on a different side of the frame after the camera moves. Similarly, a seated character can turn their head, torso or gaze without changing seats. Describe persistent placement relative to recognizable set features, then describe the framing and attention needed from this camera position.

In a conversation, know both where each person is and whom they are looking at. Their positions alone do not determine a correct eyeline. Before a reverse angle, check the line of interaction, camera side, gaze target, visibility of the other performer and the screen direction the edit will present. Deliberate axis changes are valid when planned and made understandable.

Internal sketches and labels can help planning. Use the established character/Element names and visible relationships in final prompts; do not send unexplained map coordinates or temporary codes to the model. Several views or a diagram help describe a set but do not create a guaranteed 3D environment or persistent actor coordinates.

## Inspect the images that the plan depends on

A planned asset is not evidence of the image produced. Inspect the actual reference before using it to claim a position, light source, entrance or movement path. Follow [platform access](platform-access.md) for authorized read-only viewing, and [image asset methods](image-asset-methods.md) for making or revising assets.

For references intended to show one location, compare the fixed architecture and usable floor space. Doors, windows, major objects, scale and level changes must make sense together. Then check time of day, weather and illumination. A matching color palette does not establish a matching place.

A second angle may reveal something the first image never showed. Treat that unseen area as a design proposal requiring approval, not as a fact recovered from the first picture. Prepare the views the planned coverage needs; do not demand an exhaustive asset atlas for a simple scene.

State why each reference is being used: recognizable character, wardrobe or prop design, environment, a specific composition, or a required state of the action. Check the state as well as the design. An image after an entrance or interaction may help explain the destination while being unsuitable for the beginning. Assigning a purpose in text does not hide other conflicting content in that image, and mentioning a reference does not bind a Start Frame slot.

Where references cannot coexist, return the inconsistency to the responsible owner before producing a supposedly ready prompt. Resolve only the affected inputs. If one reference is removed, identify which required facts now lack support rather than leaving identity or placement undefined.

## Follow the action through the space

For a moving person or camera, check the route from the actual opening composition. Account for reachable surfaces, obstacles, contact points and the space the camera reveals. A change from a low view to a high view needs a coherent location throughout, not two attractive endpoints connected by an unexplained move.

An unseen performer may already be behind the camera, behind an obstacle or outside the frame; establish that presence when the intended reveal depends on it. If a person is absent from the space, they need an entrance. Background performers continue appropriate activity or stillness through the shot rather than automatically freezing to resemble a reference photograph.

Let movement retain its physical cause. Starting to walk, turning while carrying something, making contact and stopping each depend on the current posture and route. Preserve preparation or aftermath needed for the story or edit. Use the physical-action guidance in [prompt and shot guide](prompt-and-shot-guide.md) for more detailed failures.

Dialogue must also fit the space. Check who can see and hear an event, who is addressing whom, and whether turning or movement changes that relationship. Requested interruptions, laughter and simultaneous responses remain part of the action. For the entry cue, voice intelligibility and listener timing, use [actor performance](actor-performance-and-facial-actions.md); do not add a competing turn-taking rule here.

A blocking sketch or a brief manual walk-through of the proposed camera and subject paths can expose contradictions before generation. If a media test would materially resolve an uncertain route, propose a narrowly scoped test and what it would establish. It is optional, has no prescribed duration, and does not authorize generation or spending credits. Current platform-specific preview tools must be verified before being recommended as available.

## Keep light decisions consistent across the five roles

The responsibilities are distinct even though each role uses the same scene intention:

- **① Directing:** approve the overall visual character and the scene's time, meaningful light sources, dominant direction and intended exposure/contrast relationships. Identify any motivated change during the scene.
- **② Assets:** realize those decisions in the relevant location and composition images, then record what is actually visible. A neutral character portrait establishes identity; its background and studio illumination need not become the scene's environment or light source.
- **③ Shot execution:** express the necessary lighting from the actual camera angle and for the current action. Select only verified controls compatible with the approved intention and inspected references.
- **④ Review:** compare the observed shadows, reflections, illuminated faces and transitions with the approved scene and adjacent selected material. Separate an observed conflict from a suspected cause.
- **⑤ Editing:** match the selected material's exposure, color and contrast where feasible, and report joins that need another solution. Grading cannot be assumed to fix contradictory shadows or create a missing physical source.

A lamp fixed in the room can illuminate a different screen side in the reverse angle. Preserve its world position and useful relationships rather than repeating a screen-left lighting phrase for every setup. A motivated fill or shot-level adjustment may preserve the look; changing the scene's time or dominant source requires a directing decision.

No universal rule makes prose, an image or a Studio setting the winner. If a selected setting contradicts the approved scene, resolve the conflict before calling the prompt ready. Keep selectable names with the controls and necessary visible behavior with the shot instruction. Use Auto or neutral only where the chosen interface actually offers a suitable option. Do not restate a full lighting plan when the current inputs already communicate it reliably.

## Describe only detail that can matter in the frame

For broad framing, prioritize silhouette, material contrast, posture and visible state. A close view can use regional surface information such as damp cloth, a tear line or a localized highlight when it matters. An extreme detail view should have a clear subject instead of a full-body inventory.

An accepted asset normally supplies established appearance. Add only the state change the shot needs, such as wetness after contact with water or exertion affecting clothing and breath. A blurred source face does not become a reliable identity reference because the prompt asks for greater resolution. Repeated quality slogans are not substitutes for an inspected image and coherent light.

## Hand the next shot an observable state

Separate what the shot is intended to end on from what the footage actually provides. Check the selected outgoing position, direction of movement, held object, line or breath and the relevant light. Role ⑤ chooses the edit interval; ③'s planned endpoint is not an actual out-point.

A new angle can preserve place and performance without duplicating the prior composition. Conversely, two nearly identical images do not ensure a usable cut: compare the actual pose, motion phase, eyeline, expression, sound and timing. Choose coverage for the intended edit rather than making every next clip begin with a newly synthesized copy of the previous frame.

When a join fails, identify whether the problem is in the plan, asset, generated action or selected edit. Route the change accordingly. A mutually inconsistent reference set needs an asset correction before more prompt polishing; a usable alternative edit may need no regeneration. Preserve accepted work outside the affected dependency.
