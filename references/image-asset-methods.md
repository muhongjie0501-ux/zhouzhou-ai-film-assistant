# Image assets for an approved shot plan

Role ② uses this guide to supply the visual information a planned film shot still lacks. Begin with the approved plan and the pictures actually available. The deliverable is a useful, reviewed reference, not a prescribed number of pictures. Use [prompt-delivery.md](prompt-delivery.md) for English/Chinese prompts and Element syntax, and [project-workflow.md](project-workflow.md) for records and handoff. These instructions grant no upload or generation authority.

## Establish what the next shot needs to know

Read the relevant shot brief and inspect the effective asset versions. Decide which uncertainty would prevent ③ from writing a reliable shot: a face that cannot be identified, an unseen side of the set, the position of a door, a prop's operating parts, or the opening arrangement of several people. Existing evidence may already answer it; request an image only for the remaining need.

An identity reference can establish a person's face without deciding their emotional response. A wardrobe reference can establish clothing without establishing a readable face. A location view establishes only the geometry it shows. A composition reference can show people in place without being bound to a platform Start Frame. Assign use according to those visible limits, not the filename or the prompt that produced the picture.

Where a picture supports several needs, record that use only after inspection. Role assignment does not isolate the model from the rest of the image: a useful face on an incompatible background can still create a conflict. Resolve important contradictions with [performance-space-methods.md](performance-space-methods.md) before writing a dependent prompt.

## Design the missing information at the scale of use

Work back from the planned view. A distant building needs a recognizable silhouette and openings; a close view of its handle needs the handle's shape, mounting and condition. Do not spend a prompt on details that the intended shot cannot resolve. For a person, prioritize the identifying features visible at that shot size and preserve the approved appearance.

Use an object's construction to make its appearance coherent. State what it is made of, how it has been finished or worn, and the visible features that distinguish it. Add surface detail when it helps recognition or explains an interaction. A rubber grip and a polished metal clasp should respond differently to the same scene light; generic claims of premium texture do not describe that difference.

Take the scene's time, sources of illumination and approved visual treatment from ①. Role ② makes those decisions visible in the asset; it does not approve a replacement lighting scheme. Describe the sides or surfaces lit by the existing sources and the required readable detail. Keep an identity portrait's neutral light separate from a scene reference's light. Use the palette established for the scene without imposing a fixed color ratio.

Resolve the intended medium when first writing the prompt. A live-action target needs instructions consistent with photographic appearance; an illustrated target needs instructions consistent with its chosen technique. A layout sketch can be used for spatial information while the requested result remains photographic. State that limited use rather than inheriting the sketch's rendering style. Focus and depth should serve the asset's purpose: a geography view may require both a near entrance and a far path to be legible, while a detail view may isolate one object. There is no universal lens or composition recipe.

## Preserve the set when the viewpoint changes

A different camera angle changes what is visible and where it appears on screen; it does not move the actors or rebuild the location. Start from the established relationships among people, landmarks and light sources. Locate the proposed camera relative to them, then derive the visible front/back sides, overlaps, foreground and background. Keep body orientation and gaze distinct from screen position.

For reverse coverage, use [dialogue-coverage-grammar.md](dialogue-coverage-grammar.md). Do not mirror an image to stand in for a reverse angle: asymmetric set details and lighting would also reverse. Preserve the established location of each person unless the approved action moves them.

A wall hidden in every available view remains unknown. Propose its design only if the authorized shot needs it, obtain the applicable creative decision, and keep that proposal separate from inspected evidence. Several coherent views can support practical continuity, but do not constitute a measured or verified 3D reconstruction.

Choose views from coverage needs, including entrances, exits and action routes. A contact sheet or additional angle is useful only if its panels are consistent and large enough to inspect. Do not require a standard multi-view pack or a new opening still for every generation. A stable existing reference set may support several angles; a precise contact, occlusion or group arrangement may justify one additional composition image.

A still describes one moment. If the story changes a prop, costume or environment, distinguish the state needed at the opening from a state reached later. Make a separate state image when its appearance must be exact, reused, or has already proved difficult. Do not use a visibly damaged object as evidence for its undamaged opening state.

## Treat readable story information as content

When the audience must read an inscription or recognize a recurring symbol, use the approved wording or design. Keep the quoted inscription unchanged across the English and Chinese prompt versions unless the director requests a translated on-screen text. Specify where it appears, its orientation and a readable size in the intended view. Add letter style, weight, color or mark geometry only when those features matter to the story or continuity.

Keep the approved inscription or design version in the asset record already used by ②. Inspect the actual result against it: words, punctuation and distinguishing strokes, not just the overall impression of a sign. Unreadable pixels remain unverified. Pass the approved version and any limitations to ③; ④ reviews its consistency in the material actually seen. This method does not promise correct model lettering or require legible incidental background writing.

## Revise an image without silently changing the shot

Use the actual effective image as the starting evidence. Identify the requested difference and the anchors the request leaves in force, such as identity, camera position or set geometry. An edit must also accommodate physical consequences: replacing a transparent panel can change reflections, what is seen through it and the light reaching nearby surfaces. Preserve meaningful continuity rather than demanding that every old pixel survive.

For removal, identify the surface or space that should become visible. Use existing evidence where available; an unseen structure is a design question, not something recovered from the original. For an angle change or major redesign, describe the work honestly rather than presenting it as a small local touch-up. Choose a route appropriate to the scope only after its capabilities are known; this guide imposes no model ranking.

After the user generates an edit, inspect both the requested difference and the important unchanged regions. If the change spread to the face, set or framing, identify that failure before writing a longer prompt. Editing instructions are targets to test, not guarantees of pixel identity.

## Review for the next role

Review the actual pixels through the authorized platform or supplied-file route. Do not automatically download another copy. Check the information this asset was requested to supply, its compatibility with the other active references, and any critical unknowns. Keep suitability advice separate from the director's acceptance.

When a photographic target instead looks illustrated, compare the approved medium with the prompt, reference purposes and selected controls. A conflicting rendering instruction is one possible cause; lighting, materials or the reference itself may also explain the result. Correct the supported cause while preserving the approved composition and design. Intentional animation, painting and mixed media remain valid; there is no permanent banned-word list. Reinspect the result before declaring the problem solved.

Give ③ the effective reference, its observed view and state, its useful information and its limitations through the existing asset record. A planned name, an existing image, a saved Element and an asset bound on the current page are different states. If essential geometry or text has not been verified, keep the dependent shot conditional. Route a necessary staging or look change back to ① and provide the actual result to ④; do not restart unaffected assets.
